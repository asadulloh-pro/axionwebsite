# Axionlab home page: scenario and copy (English, v1)

One idea runs through the whole page: **many devices, one picture, one decision.**

The visitor is treated as an operator. Each chapter is one screen and one thought. Text is short; the visuals carry the explanation.

All copy below is new. Nothing is taken from the reference sites. Visuals marked "illustrative" use sample data and are not real Axionlab screens.

Numbers: the page uses no performance figures (latency, range, uptime, number of deployments). Counts that appear (for example "six device types") describe the story on the page, not a product claim. Real figures can be added once confirmed.

---

## 0. Prologue: before the signal

**Visual:** dark night terrain, a quiet radar grid with a slow sweep. A few faint, unconnected blips.

- Kicker: `Axionlab · Device integration · C2 / C4 software`
- Headline: **Every device speaks its own language.**
- Subline: Radars, lidars, cameras, jammers and drones watch the same sky. They just can't hear each other. We make them work as one.
- Scroll cue: `Scroll to begin`

## 1. The problem: alone on separate screens

**Visual (pinned):** six separate device panels light up one by one, each showing its own signal. Their readings never line up.

- Label: `01 · The problem`
- Headline: **Six sensors. Six screens. No single answer.**
- Body: A radar sees a track. A camera sees a shape. An RF sensor hears a signal. Each one is right, and each one is alone. Operators lose minutes stitching it together by hand, and minutes are what they don't have.
- Panel tags (illustrative, same six devices as chapter 2): `RADAR · TRACK 041`, `LIDAR · 3D POINTS`, `EO/IR · OBJECT ?`, `RF · 2.4 GHz`, `UAV · TELEMETRY`, `IOT · ZONE 3 ALERT`
- Closing line: Six readings. Zero agreement.

## 2. Connect anything

**Visual (pinned):** an integration core at the centre. Each device icon draws a link into it as you scroll, one at a time.

- Label: `02 · Integration`
- Headline: **If it senses or acts, we connect it.**
- Body: Axionlab builds the layer between hardware and decision. A new radar, a thermal camera, a jammer from another vendor: each device gets an adapter, and its data joins one common model.
- Devices:
  - **Radar**: tracks, range, bearing and speed.
  - **Lidar**: point clouds and precise 3D geometry.
  - **EO/IR camera**: video, thermal imagery and detections.
  - **RF sensor and jammer**: signal detection and controlled response.
  - **UAV**: telemetry, payload video and mission control.
  - **IoT and telemetry**: perimeter, environment and system health.
- Footnote: Vendor-neutral by design. Built around the equipment you already operate.

## 3. One operational picture

**Visual (pinned, illustrative):** scattered markers from different sensors drift together on a map and fuse into a few clean tracks. A track list fills in beside the map.

- Label: `03 · Fusion`
- Headline: **Many signals. One picture.**
- Body: Data from every device is correlated and placed on one map. A radar track and a camera detection become one object, with one ID, one history and one level of confidence.
- Sample tracks (illustrative): `TRK-0217 · UAV · RADAR + EO + RF`, `TRK-0218 · VEHICLE · LIDAR + EO`, `TRK-0221 · UNKNOWN · RADAR`
- Caption: `Illustrative interface · sample data`

## 4. Decide: command and control

**Visual (pinned):** a four-step timeline. The target panel changes with each step: a blip, a classified box, an operator confirmation, a response.

- Label: `04 · C2 / C4`
- Headline: **From detection to decision.**
- Steps:
  1. **Detect**: sensors pick up an object and a track is created automatically.
  2. **Classify**: computer vision and ML models suggest what it is and how certain they are.
  3. **Confirm**: the operator sees all the evidence in one place and makes the call.
  4. **Respond**: the command goes to the right device, from a camera to a jammer, and every step is logged.
- Closing line: Machines handle the speed. People keep the authority.

## 5. Built for the field

**Visual:** four large word cards appear in sequence. No numbers.

- Label: `05 · Engineering`
- Headline: **Designed for the conditions, not the demo.**
- Cards:
  - **Real-time**: streams are processed as they arrive, for work where seconds matter.
  - **Resilient**: keeps operating on unstable links and degraded infrastructure.
  - **Secure**: protection is part of every integration, transfer and store.
  - **Scalable**: a distributed architecture that grows from one post to a network.

## 6. Where it works

**Visual:** two large panels with code-built visuals (radar arcs for defense, an infrastructure network for civil).

- Label: `06 · Missions`
- Headline: **Two missions. One platform.**
- Defense and security: C2 and C4 systems for armed forces and security services. Airspace monitoring, perimeter and border protection, counter-drone operations.
- Critical infrastructure: monitoring and control for energy sites, transport, industry and cities that need a live, trustworthy picture.
- Link: `Explore solutions`

## 7. The stack

**Visual:** a compact list; each row lights up as it enters view.

- Label: `07 · Technology`
- Headline: **The engineering underneath.**
- Rows:
  - **Computer vision**: detection and tracking on video and thermal streams.
  - **Machine learning**: models trained and tuned on real sensor data.
  - **Simulation**: virtual scenarios to test systems before the field does.
  - **Real-time processing**: live streams handled with immediate response.
  - **Distributed systems**: high-load architecture for many devices and sites.
  - **IoT and telemetry**: continuous data from sensors and equipment.
- Link: `All technologies`
